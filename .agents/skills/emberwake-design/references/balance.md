# Balance

## BAL-001 — Compare like situations

Name the build, encounter, actor stats, equipment, and starting state that matter. Compare candidate and baseline under comparable conditions. Use matched random seeds where supported and vary them when randomness could reverse the result. Compare the effect and its opportunity cost in attack and recovery time, stamina, or exposure to enemy attacks. For effects with trade-offs, keep measures such as damage, prevention, control, and success rate separate unless the design supplies a conversion.

## BAL-002 — Check whether a choice crowds out another

Compare reactions and stronger actions against relevant basic commands in the same encounter. Record actual opportunities and decisions as well as successful triggers. For a protection reaction, inspect when Guard would have been useful, when it was chosen, and how often the reaction covered the same need. When testing a cap, check multiple eligible events, resolution order, and reset. Change one lever where practical and repeat the comparison in another situation where the baseline choice should have a role before drawing broader conclusions.

## BAL-003 — Separate calculation, playtest, and acceptance

Show the assumptions and units for calculations. Tie observations to the sampled encounters. A report that a change felt better can guide the next check. A balance claim needs evidence about the specific trade-off being asserted. Keep candidate numbers attached to the build or proposal until the user adopts them.
